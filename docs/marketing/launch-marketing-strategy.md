# Unicorn Viz x DJ Unicorn Tears Launch Marketing Strategy

Owner: Studio Marketing
Status: active
Last updated: 2026-06-03

## Mission

Launch Unicorn Viz and DJ Unicorn Tears as a single entertainment and creator-product ecosystem with three outcomes:

1. A world-premiere livestream event people want to attend live and replay later.
2. A clear free-to-paid funnel for Unicorn Viz core software, premium drop-ins, music, merch, and memberships.
3. A repeatable content machine that can keep growing for at least 60 days after the premiere.

This campaign is built around a 30-day pre-premiere runway, a 6-day live event block, and a 60-day post-premiere growth cycle.

## Working Assumptions

- Unicorn Viz core software remains free.
- Most drop-ins are paid products and should be framed as premium expansion packs.
- DJ Unicorn Tears is the flagship on-camera identity for the launch.
- 10% of gross proceeds are donated to animals in need and should be treated as a permanent brand promise.
- The official homes are unicorntears.com and unicornviz.com.
- OBS is not fully production-ready yet, so the livestream plan must support both OBS-based workflows and Unicorn Viz native streaming.

## Positioning

### Core Position

Unicorn Viz is a performance-grade visual instrument for live electronic artists, streamers, and video creators.

### Artist Position

DJ Unicorn Tears is the first full expression of the software: original music, high-energy visuals, and a strong on-stream persona that makes the tool feel alive.

### Offer Ladder

- Free: Unicorn Viz core download, teaser tracks, highlight clips, wallpapers, email signup rewards.
- Entry paid: premiere digital pack, first paid drop-ins, remix pack, low-ticket merch.
- Mid-tier paid: bundles of premium drop-ins, curated visual packs, premium replay archive, behind-the-scenes content.
- Recurring paid: membership tiers for early access, exclusive hangouts, private drops, production notes, and member-only stream archives.

## Audience Segments

- Music fans who want DJ sets, remixes, livestreams, and artist identity.
- Creator-operators who want visual software, paid drop-ins, and setup inspiration.
- Community supporters who want direct access, private streams, and a mission-driven brand to back.
- Collectors who buy limited merch, launch bundles, and premiere-only digital products.

## Campaign Architecture

The campaign should always move people through four steps:

1. Discovery through clips, teasers, collaborator mentions, and platform-native short-form.
2. Capture through email, Substack, site visits, and event RSVP/waitlist flows.
3. Conversion through free core software, premiere bundles, paid drop-ins, merch, and subscriptions.
4. Retention through post-premiere streams, serialized content, supporter-only access, and new paid drops.

## 30-Day Pre-Premiere Strategy

### Phase 1: Foundation and Setup (T-30 to T-24)

Goals:

- Lock the launch narrative.
- Build the funnel infrastructure before traffic arrives.
- Create the first content bank.

Actions:

- Finalize naming, bios, profile art, headers, avatars, and handles across every platform.
- Publish or stage launch landing pages on unicorntears.com and unicornviz.com.
- Build a single link path for all bios with route-specific tracking.
- Set up Fourthwall products, digital delivery, donation language, and animal-support messaging.
- Decide the default membership stack: Substack for editorial/email, Locals for hangouts, SubscribeStar as backup recurring support.
- Finalize the free launch offer and the first premium drop-in bundle.
- Cut the first 12 to 20 short-form assets before public cadence starts.
- Test both OBS and native Unicorn Viz streaming paths with scene templates, audio routing, overlays, and backup recordings.

### Phase 2: Reveal and Story Build (T-23 to T-17)

Goals:

- Introduce the world and make the artist/software pairing memorable.
- Start list building and profile growth.

Actions:

- Release the DJ Unicorn Tears intro/theme audio paired with a 15 to 30 second hero teaser.
- Publish a launch post on Substack explaining the project, world premiere, and the animal donation commitment.
- Drop short clips that each highlight one angle: music identity, visual identity, free software, premium drop-ins, and mission.
- Start daily countdown language on X, Facebook Stories, YouTube Shorts, and vertical clips for all mirrored channels.
- Reach out to creators, DJs, visual artists, and niche press with the one-sheet and creator pack.
- Seed one free gated reward for email or Substack signups such as wallpapers, a preview loop pack, or a teaser mix.

### Phase 3: Proof and Conversion Warm-Up (T-16 to T-10)

Goals:

- Show that the project is real, polished, and imminent.
- Train the audience on what to do next.

Actions:

- Publish software demo clips that clearly separate free core features from paid drop-ins.
- Share behind-the-scenes build footage, artist prep, and micro-devlog material.
- Announce the six-day livestream structure with themes for each day.
- Open merch previews, product waitlists, and supporter tier previews.
- Run one small community hangout on Locals to test live interaction and gather language that resonates.
- Put the premiere countdown timer on both official sites and in pinned social posts.

### Phase 4: Countdown and Commitment (T-9 to T-1)

Goals:

- Convert passive interest into scheduled attendance.
- Make the audience feel they are joining an event, not just consuming content.

Actions:

- Pin the official trailer and event announcement everywhere.
- Publish daily countdown posts with one unique asset per day rather than repeating one graphic.
- Rotate calls to action between RSVP, follow, free core download, merch waitlist, and supporter signup.
- Release one final high-impact trailer 72 hours before day one.
- Publish the complete six-day schedule with platform links, time zones, and replay expectations.
- Run a final technical rehearsal with recording, backup ingest, scene changes, and emergency fallback workflows.

## Baseline Weekly Cadence During Pre-Premiere

- Short-form clips: 2 per day across priority channels, adapted natively rather than blindly cross-posted.
- Long-form video: 2 per week, alternating between set/demo and story/build content.
- Editorial/email: 2 per week via Substack.
- Community post: 3 per week across Locals and Facebook.
- Livestream: 1 test or casual warm-up stream per week until the six-day block begins.

## Six-Day World Premiere Livestream Block

Treat the six-day run as a festival week, not six copies of the same show.

| Day | Theme | Core format | Primary platform emphasis | Main monetization CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Arrival | Opening spectacle, artist origin, platform kickoff set | YouTube + Twitch + Kick + mirrored archive targets | Join mailing list and follow everywhere |
| Day 2 | Build | Behind-the-scenes walkthrough, visual system breakdown, Q&A | YouTube + Substack recap + Locals thread | Download Unicorn Viz core |
| Day 3 | Fire | High-energy DJ set with premium drop-in showcases | Twitch + Kick + Rumble replay | Buy premiere drop-in bundle |
| Day 4 | Community | Guest hangout, fan chat, supporter stream, creative feedback | Locals + Twitch + Facebook community push | Join memberships |
| Day 5 | Commerce | Merch reveal, digital bundle spotlight, remix/sample preview | YouTube + X + Fourthwall landing push | Buy merch and digital packs |
| Day 6 | World Premiere Finale | Signature hero set with strongest visuals and donation push | Simulcast everywhere with official replay homes on YouTube and Rumble | Buy, tip, subscribe, support animals |

Operational rules for the six-day run:

- Use one daily flagship CTA only. Secondary links can exist, but the stream script must keep repeating one primary ask.
- Publish same-day recap clips within 2 to 6 hours after each stream ends.
- Send one Substack recap after each of the first three days and one final recap after day six.
- Save the best 20 to 40 second clips for the final two days rather than exhausting highlights early.
- Mention the 10% animal support commitment once per stream block and once in every recap email.
- If OBS becomes unstable, fall back immediately to native Unicorn Viz streaming plus clean local recording.

## 60-Day Post-Premiere Strategy

### Days 1-14: Capture and Replay Window

Goals:

- Turn live viewers into subscribers, customers, and email contacts.
- Maximize replay value while attention is still hot.

Actions:

- Publish highlight edits, replay chapters, top moments, and platform-native cuts.
- Release the free starter pack prominently to convert casual viewers.
- Open the first premium bundle for a limited launch window.
- Send a post-premiere survey to email subscribers, buyers, and community members.
- Clip testimonials, chat reactions, and creator comments into social proof posts.

### Days 15-30: Monetization and Product Education

Goals:

- Turn curiosity into product understanding.
- Prove that paid drop-ins and memberships continue to deliver value.

Actions:

- Run weekly product showcases for specific drop-ins or bundle themes.
- Publish tutorial content that helps free users become paying power users.
- Launch one supporter-only or member-first stream every week.
- Release one remix, sample, or behind-the-scenes asset to keep the music side active.
- Test bundle pricing, limited-time offers, and merch pairings.

### Days 31-60: Expansion and Retention

Goals:

- Move from launch mode into an ongoing release calendar.
- Build recurring revenue instead of relying on one-time spikes.

Actions:

- Establish a predictable weekly schedule for one live set, one product piece, one editorial piece, and daily short-form.
- Launch the next drop-in or content pack before the premiere buzz fully cools.
- Bring in collaborators, remixes, guest sets, or creator showcases.
- Segment email and supporter audiences by interest: music, visuals, merch, and insider access.
- Promote case studies and creator outcomes to sell Unicorn Viz as a serious tool, not just a novelty stream backdrop.

## Content Pillars

- Performance: livestream sets, edited replays, hero trailers, visual-only moments.
- Product: demos, feature breakdowns, free-vs-paid comparisons, tutorials.
- Personality: DJ Unicorn Tears voice, mission, humor, behind-the-scenes, studio prep.
- Community: hangouts, polls, questions, remixes, reactions, supporter recognition.
- Cause: animal-support updates connected to proceeds without making the charity message feel transactional.

## Monetization Model

- Free software as the top-of-funnel acquisition engine.
- Paid drop-ins as the main digital revenue driver.
- Fourthwall storefront as the main merch and digital bundle retail surface until custom site flows are fully mature.
- Memberships split by role: Substack for editorial loyalty, Locals for live community, SubscribeStar as recurring revenue redundancy.
- Buy Me a Coffee, PayPal, and Rumble Wallet for direct support and low-friction tipping.

## Measurement and Adaptation Loop

Review performance every 72 hours during pre-premiere and every 48 hours during the six-day livestream run.

Track:

- Reach: impressions, unique viewers, site sessions, profile visits.
- Engagement: watch time, average view duration, comments, shares, saves, live chat rate.
- Capture: email signups, Substack subscribers, Locals joins, Discord-equivalent interest if added later.
- Conversion: free downloads, bundle sales, merch sales, supporter joins, direct tips.
- Revenue mix: software-related sales, merch, music, memberships, tips, donation contribution.

Decision triggers:

- If clips generate reach but not clicks, rewrite CTAs and simplify landing pages.
- If site traffic converts but social reach is weak, shift energy into creator partnerships and repost-friendly short-form.
- If merch lags but digital products move, bundle merch with digital bonuses instead of discounting too early.
- If live streams hold viewers but post-stream sales are weak, tighten the offer presentation and same-day recap funnels.
- If one platform materially outperforms the others, keep posting everywhere but let the winning platform set hero content format.

## Required Assets and Operations

- Brand kit with avatars, banners, lower thirds, donation lockup, and thumbnail templates.
- Premiere trailer, daily countdown graphics, and six daily stream key art variants.
- Theme song master, stream intro audio reel, clean stingers, and cutdown-safe music excerpts.
- OBS scenes, native Unicorn Viz streaming presets, recording presets, and backup routing instructions.
- Fourthwall store pages, digital bundle delivery flow, and clear product naming that distinguishes free core from paid drop-ins.
- Email/Substack templates for countdown, day-of alerts, recaps, and post-premiere offers.
- A shared operating sheet for handles, links, post status, assets, owners, and metrics.

## Team Operating Roles

- Creative lead owns narrative, visual consistency, and headline campaign moments.
- Artist lead owns on-stream performance, voice, and audience intimacy.
- Product lead owns Unicorn Viz feature framing, free-versus-paid positioning, and docs tie-ins.
- Growth lead owns posting cadence, analytics, paid boosts if used, and platform learning.
- Commerce lead owns Fourthwall, memberships, support channels, and promotional offer timing.
- Production lead owns stream tech, OBS/native fallback, audio routing, recording, and replay packaging.
